## Question

When a listening impression and a measurement disagree, which one wins?

## Position

Neither wins alone. A graph can tell us where the bass sits and whether the left and right sides match. It cannot tell us whether a voice sounds like a person. Listening can tell us that, but it cannot tell us why, and it changes with mood, volume and the last thing we heard.

So the plan is to use them in order. We listen to decide what we want. We measure to find out what we actually built, and to make sure the next unit is the same as the last.

## Why not tune to a graph

It is easy to chase a curve that looks good on a chart. A monitor can measure well and still be tiring after an hour. We would rather publish a plain-looking graph of something we enjoy than a handsome graph of something we don't.

## Decided

- Every tuning change gets both a listening note and a measurement before it is kept.
- Measurements go on the spec sheet only when they are repeatable.

## Open

- The target sound. We have not published one because we have not settled it.
- The measurement rig. Results depend heavily on the equipment, and we will name ours when we report numbers.
